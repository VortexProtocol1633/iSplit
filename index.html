About iSplit & Demo

iSplit turns oversized images and PDFs into neatly tiled pages you can print on any standard printer — privately, in your browser.

Guided demo (how to use the buttons)

Real screenshots of iSplit, captured while walking through each button below.

Features & use cases

  • Images & PDFs: JPG/PNG and multi-page PDFs (choose a page). All local.
  • Segments, Manual, or Auto grid: Pick a total segment count, exact rows × columns, or auto-calculate from a poster size.
  • Clear paper/orientation: Standard/custom sizes, units, and an explicit Portrait (taller) / Landscape (wider) toggle with dimensions.
  • Precise control: Zoom (slider or mouse wheel), pan, crop, rotate, margins, gap, overlap, borders, crop marks, numbers, cut guides.
  • Clickable tiles & quality output: Exclude tiles, set DPI/JPEG quality, export multi-page PDF or print with correct @page sizing.
  • Use cases: Posters, banners, blueprints, maps, wall art, sewing/craft patterns. Tip: print at 100% / Actual size and use overlap for assembly.

Privacy

Files are processed locally with in-browser libraries and never sent anywhere. Settings are stored only in your browser’s local storage (use Reset to clear).

From the Creator

Md Masrur Masuk Shopnil

Engineer by training · designer by stubbornness

Hi — I'm the person behind iSplit. I did not go to design school; I studied at MIST (Military Institute of Science and Technology, Bangladesh), which taught me a lot about engineering and approximately nothing about colour theory. Everything you see here is self-taught, mildly obsessive, hobbyist-grade web design. The themes were mostly me asking “does this indigo go with this cyan?” and then reloading the page forty times.

What I do know is the special kind of tedium that comes from needing one poster on a printer that only takes A4. The artwork is ninety centimetres wide, the print dialog insists on scaling it to fit, and by the time you have cut and taped six pages together the seams are off by a few millimetres and the title has a stripe through it. One day I got fed up enough to build a tool instead of complaining about it.

iSplit takes whatever you have — a JPG, a PNG, a multi-page PDF — and slices it into clean, printable tiles at the paper size you actually own, with margins, overlap and crop marks so the pieces line up. It previews the grid live, lets you switch off the tiles you don't need, and exports the whole thing as one PDF or straight to your printer. Like a very patient print technician who never trims the wrong edge.

No accounts, no server, no database. Everything happens in your browser, and your files never leave your machine. It is the smallest tool that does the job, which is roughly the only kind of tool I trust with a 200-megapixel scan of somebody's family photo.

It started as a weekend fix for my own wall map and quietly turned into… this. I hope it saves you as much time (and hand-cramp) as it's saved me.
